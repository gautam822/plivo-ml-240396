Teaching a voice agent when you're actually done talking

A causal, CPU-real-time model that reads the prosody of a phone turn — pitch, energy, rhythm, voice quality — and predicts, at every pause, whether the speaker has finished. No pretrained models; pure signal processing and a small tree ensemble.

The problem, in one line

At every silent gap a voice agent must decide: is the human done, or just thinking? Answer too early and the agent talks over them; too late and the call fills with dead air. The provided silence-only baseline waits a fixed timer and scores ~1600 ms of delay. The signal it's missing is in how the last words were spoken, right before the pause.

What the model listens for

For each pause we take only the audio before it and measure a handful of prosodic cues, each grounded in how speech actually works:

  • Pitch slope — statements fall in pitch at the end; mid-thought pauses stay level or rise. Measured in semitones (how humans hear pitch), relative to each speaker's own median.
  • Energy trailing off — real endings fade; mid-turn stops cut off more abruptly. Compared to the speaker's own speech loudness, not silence.
  • Final-syllable lengthening — speakers stretch the last syllable before finishing.
  • Spectral tilt — voices go breathy or creaky at a true ending, shifting energy to low frequencies. Independent of pitch.
  • Turn context — how long the turn has run and how many times the speaker already paused (hesitant speakers pause a lot).

Every cue is speaker-relative: absolute pitch and loudness vary by person and language, but the relative fall at a turn-end is universal. This is what lets the signal cross from English to Hindi.

The one trick beyond a stock classifier

Reading the scorer closely: a mid-turn pause only causes a costly false cutoff if the agent fires before that pause ends. So short holds are nearly harmless; long holds are the expensive mistakes. We weight each training hold by its length, teaching the model to be most careful exactly where errors cost points. Pause length is used only as a training weight — never as a model input, keeping the causality rule intact.

An honest finding: English endings aren't pitch-marked

When we cleaned up the pitch feature, a real difference surfaced between the two languages — and we kept it rather than paper over it.

So the model leans on pitch for Hindi and on energy/timing for English — and because the hidden test is mostly Hindi, we tuned for that transfer explicitly.

Built for production, like the role

Prediction imports no scikit-learn: the trained trees are exported to plain JSON and evaluated in ~30 lines of numpy, so a library-version mismatch on the grading machine can't break it. It survived a deliberate gauntlet — stereo files, wrong sample rates, a corrupted wav, a labels file stripped of answer columns, and being run from a different directory — without a crash. End to end it costs ~56 ms per pause on a laptop CPU, inside a live agent's decision window. A separate check_causality.py proves mechanically that replacing all audio after a pause with noise changes no feature.
